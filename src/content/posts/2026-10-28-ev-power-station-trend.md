What people are discussing on X is an electric vehicle used as a power source, not only as a car. This desk opened two trending pages on 28 October 2026. One is titled ["Tesla Powershare Turns Every New Car into Portable Power Station"](https://x.com/i/trending/2115196106531369380). X calls it a summary of posts, says the summary may change, and showed a last-updated line of 28 October 2026. The other is titled ["Ford's Affordable Fathom EV Truck Powers Homes Without Extra Gear"](https://x.com/i/trending/2115094792291876961), last updated 27 October 2026, with the same kind of label. The pages show that the topic is trending. They are not the source for the hardware facts below. The Powershare summary includes a power rating and an adapter price. This desk could not open tesla.com to confirm either number, so neither is repeated here. Other lines in that summary are not used.

The company post under the Powershare topic is quoted by [Tesla North](https://teslanorth.com/2026/10/27/tesla-powershare-outlets-canada-misses-out/) from Tesla North America's account on 27 October 2026. Tesla North America said: "Every new Tesla can now power your tools or gadgets via Powershare." Tesla North says a follow-up set two limits. The setup uses the Mobile Connector and an Outlet Adapter, and it is available in the United States and Mexico only. Canada is not included. Tesla has not said whether or when Canada will get the same option, and Tesla North does not give a timeline. Tesla North describes Powershare as Tesla's name for drawing energy from the vehicle battery for outside loads, and says Tuesday's posts extend that outlet idea to every new Tesla in the markets that qualify.

The Ford side of the same X discussion is a truck, not a connector kit. Ford's [From the Road post](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/us/en/articles/2026/fathom-ev-bidirectional-home-power), dated 27 October 2026, says the 2027 Fathom starts at an MSRP of $28,350, will be available in 2027, and can supply over a projected 10 kW of AC power to a home without a separate wall-mounted inverter. Ford says a Home Power Station, expected to cost about half as much as its first-generation bidirectional charger, is the hardware for shifting household electricity, and that the customer needs an active plan or a trial. Backup also needs a Home Backup Power Panel, expected to cost at least 60 percent less than Ford's first-generation home backup hardware. Ford's post does not set a dollar price. The X summary adds a comparison with the F-150 Lightning and a preorder date. This story does not take those claims from the summary. Where TechCrunch and The Verge disagree on the hardware dollars is covered in [Ford says the 2027 Fathom can power a home without a wall inverter](/articles/2026-10-28-ford-fathom-home-power/).

The two posts are not describing one product. Tesla North America's post, as Tesla North reports it, is about tools or gadgets from a new Tesla in the United States or Mexico. Ford's post is about sending a projected amount of AC power from a 2027 truck into a house. Neither text says the other company's system does the same job.

A federal example of home charging cost, which is not a price for either setup, is in [what it costs to charge an electric car at home](/articles/2026-10-26-ev-charging-cost/). A BMW high-power demonstration is a [separate charging story](/articles/2026-10-26-bmw-ix5-460kw-charging/).
