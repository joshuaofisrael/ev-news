Tesla owners waiting for the next big self-driving update now have a month to circle. "FSD V15 goes to wide release next month. It will be something special," [Elon Musk posted on X](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2124042864891982292) early on Saturday.

Tesla investor and commentator Sawyer Merritt [amplified the news](https://x.com/SawyerMerritt/status/2124043482683625943) minutes later: "Elon has announced that @Tesla FSD V15 will be released wide next month (December)."

## What Musk said, and what he did not

The FSD line came at the end of a post about how Tesla trains its AI. Musk wrote that supervised fine-tuning "is more like getting a haircut with a chainsaw than it is 'fine-tuning'" and that having a "system prompt" is "silly", adding: "The above are already true for Tesla AI (not yet deserving of 'super'). It is the most efficient AI on Earth."

He did not say which cars, countries or hardware versions get V15 first, what "wide" means in numbers, or what new features it brings. Tesla has not published release notes. Until it does, "something special" is a promise, not a spec.

## Read the timeline with care

"Wide release" usually means beyond Tesla's early-access testers, but Musk's dates for FSD have often slipped. December is also when Tesla usually ships its annual holiday software update, so owners should expect a busy month either way.

Whatever V15 brings, FSD in customer cars is still a supervised system: the driver has to watch the road and be ready to take over. That matters as Tesla pushes FSD into new markets in Europe and Asia, where regulators approve it country by country. Our earlier story on [how many Tesla buyers pay for FSD](/articles/2026-10-23-tesla-fsd-supervised-attach-rate/) shows why each new version matters to the company's revenue.

The thing to watch is whether V15 reaches customer cars before the end of December, and what Tesla says it can do.
