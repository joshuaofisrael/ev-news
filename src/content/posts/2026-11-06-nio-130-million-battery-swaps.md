Nio has reported a new milestone for its battery swapping network, with cumulative swaps reaching 130 million, according to [The Driven](https://thedriven.io/2026/11/05/nio-completes-10-million-ev-battery-swaps-in-just-88-days/).

The publication says the company completed its latest ten million swaps in 88 days. That pace is the central point of the milestone: the interval between successive ten million swap increments has shortened as the network and its use have grown.

## What battery swapping means here

Battery swapping is a different approach from conventional plug in charging. Rather than waiting for a vehicle’s battery to recharge, a compatible car enters a dedicated station and has its depleted pack exchanged for a charged one. The approach depends on vehicle compatibility, station availability and a sufficiently large pool of batteries.

The Driven attributes the company’s comments about the milestone to Nio founder and chief executive William Li. His statements should be treated as company claims about the network’s scale and operating progress, rather than as independently audited measures in this report.

## What the total does not show

The milestone provides a snapshot of activity across Nio’s swapping ecosystem, but the cumulative total alone does not establish how evenly stations are distributed, how often individual drivers use them or the economics of operating the network. Those questions require separate information about station locations, utilisation, battery inventory and operating costs.

The latest ten million swaps in 88 days suggest that use of the service has continued to accelerate, at least on the company’s reported count. It also illustrates the scale that a battery swapping system needs to reach if it is to offer a meaningful alternative to charging for drivers of compatible vehicles.

The available source does not provide an independent audit of the 130 million total. The figure and the 88 day interval are therefore attributed to Nio’s reported operating data.
