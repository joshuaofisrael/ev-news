Our Next Energy (ONE), the Michigan battery company, has agreed to go public by combining with Wen Acquisition Corp, a special purpose acquisition company listed on Nasdaq as WENN. The two companies announced the definitive agreement on 16 November in a [press release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2057043/000121390026120194/ea031236801ex99-1.htm). The combined company is expected to trade on Nasdaq as ONEE.

## The deal terms

According to the release, the deal gives the combined company a pro forma equity value of about $1 billion. That is made up of $600 million of pre-money equity plus up to $400 million in gross proceeds: $300 million of cash in Wen's trust, assuming no shareholders redeem, and a $100 million private placement (PIPE). Only $30 million of the PIPE is committed so far, from two strategic investors in steel and defence, and $10 million of that is under a non-binding letter of intent.

## What ONE makes

ONE makes lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells at a factory in Van Buren Township, Michigan, and assembles packs in Novi, where its pack line has 3 GWh of annual capacity. Its customers today are in defence, rail and grid storage. The company reports $40 million of cumulative revenue and more than $200 million in binding multi-year contracts, including with locomotive makers serving four Class I railroads and five defence prime contractors.

## The supply-chain pitch

Founder and chief executive Mujeeb Ijaz said LFP has grown from roughly 20% to 60% of the global battery market while China produces about 99% of the world's LFP cells. ONE argues that recent US rules restricting batteries tied to a "foreign entity of concern" make a domestically developed cell more valuable.

## What happens next

Both boards have approved the deal, which is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to shareholder votes and the SEC declaring Wen's registration statement effective. For EV buyers the link is indirect: ONE's current business is stationary and heavy-duty batteries rather than passenger cars, but more US LFP cell capacity matters for the whole battery supply chain. SPAC deals also often see high redemptions, so the final cash raised could be well below the headline figure.
