Texas finally has somewhere public to charge a Tesla Semi. "New Tesla Megacharger: Mesquite, TX (8 stalls)," [Tesla Charging posted on X](https://x.com/TeslaCharging/status/2122858332847534316). The [Tesla Semi account added](https://x.com/tesla_semi/status/2122867983819403363): "First Megacharger in Texas. We're rapidly scaling charging & service for Semi in the state."

## The site

According to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/18/tesla-semi-megacharger-network-mesquite-texas/), Tesla's map lists the station at 2175 S Town East Blvd in Mesquite, on the east side of Dallas, with eight Megachargers rated at up to 1.2 MW each. It is the first of the 19 Texas sites Tesla added to its plans in February, more than any other state, and several other Dallas to Fort Worth sites are still marked "coming soon".

## The whole public network

Electrek counts seven public Megacharger sites with 44 stalls in four states: Bloomington, Vernon and Tulare in California, Conley and Ellabell in Georgia, Hodgkins in Illinois, and now Mesquite. In early October it counted four sites with 26 stalls, so Tesla is adding roughly one site every two weeks.

## The gap to the target

Tesla's Q4 2025 shareholder report showed about 37 Megacharger sites for 2026, Electrek says, and Semi programme lead Dan Priestley has said Tesla wants 46 by early 2027. To reach 37, Tesla would need about 30 more sites in the remaining six weeks. At the current pace, Electrek estimates it ends the year with around 10. Pilot has said nine more of its sites are under construction, so a late burst is possible.

## Why it matters

Tesla began volume production of the Semi in September, and the first 50 trucks for a Chicago customer are on their way, as [we reported last week](/articles/2026-11-12-tesla-semi-first-50-trucks-chicago/). Big fleets install their own depot chargers, so most charging happens there. The public network is what lets a Semi travel beyond its home region.

## Our read

Analysis: one site every two weeks is real progress, but it is a fraction of the plan. Watch the other Texas sites marked "coming soon", because Texas freight routes are where a public truck network has to prove itself.
