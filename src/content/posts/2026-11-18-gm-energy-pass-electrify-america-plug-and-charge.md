General Motors drivers enrolled in its Energy Pass programme can now start a session at participating Electrify America stations just by plugging in, with no app, touchscreen or credit card, [GM said on 17 November](https://news.gm.com/home.detail.html/Pages/news/us/en/2026/nov/1117-charging-electrify-america-energy-pass.html).

## How it works

The driver parks at a compatible Electrify America charger and connects the car. The vehicle and charger authenticate the session automatically, the screen welcomes the driver and the charge starts. Based on internal GM testing, energy begins flowing within about 45 seconds. The session is billed through the payment method linked to Energy Pass, and session details and receipts appear in the myChevrolet, myGMC or myCadillac app. To set it up, drivers enrol in Energy Pass in one of those apps, add a payment method and follow the in-app Plug and Charge steps.

The capability is live now for GM EVs with native CCS charge ports. GM EVs with native NACS ports will get it in early 2027.

"Loading an app, reading a touchscreen, swiping a card, it all takes valuable minutes," said Wade Sheffer, vice president of GM Energy.

## A year of charging changes

GM says this builds on two earlier steps. In January it added Electrify America to its brand apps, bringing more than 6,200 Electrify America fast chargers, most of them capable of 350 kW, into the apps drivers already use. In June it launched Energy Pass, which after a one-time enrolment lets drivers find stations, start and stop sessions, pay and keep receipts in one app across networks that GM says make up about 60% of US DC fast chargers.

## What to check

The feature only covers participating Electrify America stations and drivers who have enrolled in Energy Pass. GM says Charge by App and other charging methods keep working during the rollout. Owners of newer NACS-port models will have to wait until early 2027.
