A formal US ban on Chinese cars may be weeks from becoming law. "U.S. lawmakers in Washington are closing in on a deal to ban Chinese cars from the US," Sawyer Merritt [posted on X](https://x.com/SawyerMerritt/status/2123567146382835896) on Thursday evening, citing Bloomberg. "This would prevent automakers such as BYD and Geely from entering this market."

## What is being negotiated

Bloomberg's report, [republished by The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/international/us-lawmakers-near-deal-ban-chinese-cars-spare-mercedes), says lawmakers from car-producing states in both parties want to send a bill to President Donald Trump by the end of the year. Chinese cars are already largely kept out by steep tariffs and Commerce Department rules on Chinese vehicle technology. A law would make that permanent.

The sticking point is Mercedes-Benz. A bill passed by a Senate panel earlier this year would have barred manufacturers more than 15% owned by Chinese entities from selling cars with data-transmitting technology. China's state-owned BAIC Motor owns nearly 10% of Mercedes, and Geely founder Li Shufu holds nearly another 10%, putting the German carmaker over that line.

Bloomberg says Republican Senator Bernie Moreno briefed carmakers on Wednesday about changes, including looser rules for passive stakes without voting power. Most carmaker representatives were supportive, though a Mercedes official raised concerns.

## What they are saying

"We continue to meet with all stakeholders who have a shared interest in ensuring this important legislation becomes law in this Congress," Mercedes said. Senator Katie Britt of Alabama, where Mercedes builds SUVs, said: "We understand that there will be some adjustments that have to be made, but feel good about Mercedes continuing to be able to thrive in our state and across the country."

## Why it matters

The US would be going the opposite way from Europe, where Chinese brands have grown fast, as we covered in our [story on Chinese brands overtaking Japanese ones in Europe](/articles/2026-10-27-chinese-brands-overtake-japanese-europe/). Details are still being worked out, and the bill has to pass before the new Congress starts in January.
