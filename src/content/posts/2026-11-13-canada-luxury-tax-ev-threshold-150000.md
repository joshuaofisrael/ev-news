Canada's federal government wants to take more electric cars out of its luxury tax. Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne announced on 12 November a proposal to raise the price threshold at which the tax applies to fully electric and hydrogen-powered vehicles from C$100,000 to C$150,000, [the Department of Finance said](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/11/government-of-canada-adjusts-the-taxable-value-threshold-for-zero-emission-vehicles.html). The change would take effect on 13 November, subject to later approval by Parliament.

All other vehicles would stay at the existing C$100,000 threshold. The department said the move recognises the higher upfront cost of zero-emission vehicles.

## How the tax works

The luxury tax applies to sales, imports, leases and some improvements of passenger vehicles above the threshold. The amount owed is the lesser of 10% of the vehicle's full value or 20% of the value above the threshold, according to the department. The proposal keeps that formula and only moves the threshold for eligible zero-emission vehicles, [Drive Tesla Canada notes](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/luxury-tax-ev-threshold-150000/).

On those rules, an EV priced at C$140,000 would currently owe C$8,000 (20% of the C$40,000 above C$100,000) and would owe nothing under the proposed threshold. A C$170,000 EV would see its tax fall from C$14,000 to C$4,000. These are our own illustrations of the published formula, not government examples.

## Who is affected

The change mostly matters for high-end EVs, a segment that has shrunk. Drive Tesla Canada points out that Tesla has discontinued the Model S and Model X, and works through what the change would have meant for Cybertruck prices, noting that the truck already had a separate exemption.

## Charging money in the background

The department paired the announcement with reminders of Budget 2026 spending: C$587 million over five years from 2027-28 to install more than 55,000 EV chargers through a renewed Zero Emission Vehicle Infrastructure Program, and C$45 million for charging research and development. It says Ottawa has supported more than 50,000 chargers to date.
