Tesla's supervised self-driving software has cleared another European regulator. "Mamma, butta la pasta. Now approved in Italy. Rollout begins soon," [Tesla Europe posted on X](https://x.com/teslaeurope/status/2123723334542332026) on Friday. The Italian line is a playful "Mum, put the pasta on", the kind of thing you say when you are on your way home.

## The 12th country, by one count

Sawyer Merritt, who tracks Tesla closely, [listed the European countries](https://x.com/SawyerMerritt/status/2123769655663775861) where the system is now approved: "Belgium, Italy, Netherlands, Denmark, Lithuania, Finland, Estonia, Slovenia, Czechia, Croatia, Slovakia, Latvia." He wrote that the software is "now named Tesla Assisted Driving in Europe" and that Italy marks "the 12th European country".

That list is Merritt's tally rather than an official Tesla figure, and Tesla's post did not say how many countries have signed off. It also did not give a date for the Italian rollout beyond "soon".

## Why Italy matters

Italy is one of Europe's largest car markets, so it is a bigger prize than several countries already on the list. Most of the approvals so far have come from smaller markets. Europe's biggest car markets, Germany, France and Spain, are not on Merritt's list.

In Europe, approval comes country by country, and the system is a supervised one. Drivers must keep watching the road and stay ready to take over, as they must in the US.

## What to watch

The next questions are when Italian owners actually get the update, what it costs there, and which large market follows. Tesla also says a new major version of the software is coming: Elon Musk says FSD V15 goes to wide release in December, as we report in our [FSD V15 story](/articles/2026-11-21-musk-tesla-fsd-v15-wide-release-december-something-special/).
