Los Angeles has a new kind of robotaxi on its streets. "We're moving into robotaxi testing in LA," [Zoox posted on X](https://x.com/zoox/status/2123463054939815995) on Thursday. "Rides aren't open to the public just yet, but keep an eye out for our waitlist next year."

## Who can ride

Zoox's [announcement](https://zoox.com/journal/zoox-robotaxi-in-los-angeles) says the first rollout "will be small and deliberate, starting in Santa Monica." In this early phase, rides are limited to "Zoox employees, their families, and friends." Next year the company plans to open a Los Angeles waitlist ahead of its Zoox Explorers early-rider programme.

Zoox has been mapping LA with a test fleet since spring 2025, it says, learning local driving behaviour and the city's mix of coastal roads and dense streets.

## A different kind of vehicle

Unlike the converted SUVs and sedans used by most rivals, the Zoox robotaxi was built from scratch for driverless ride-hailing. It has no steering wheel or driver's seat and carries up to four passengers in facing, carriage-style seats. Zoox says it has more than 100 safety features not found on other cars.

## The bigger rollout

Los Angeles is the latest market for the Amazon-owned company this year. Zoox says its robotaxi began driving in Austin and Miami earlier in 2026, alongside a public, now paid, service in Las Vegas and an early-rider programme in San Francisco. Last week it began employee rides in Atlanta, as we reported in our [Zoox Atlanta story](/articles/2026-11-14-zoox-robotaxi-atlanta-employee-rides/).

## Why it matters

LA is already one of the busiest robotaxi markets in the US. Zoox enters it with a purpose-built design, which may help it stand out but means it cannot scale by converting cars off a production line. The company has not said how many vehicles are in LA or when paid public rides will start.
