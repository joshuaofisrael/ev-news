After years of delays, Aptera has a finished car with a customer's name on it. "@aptera_motors has finished building its first solar electric vehicle for a paying customer, its No. 1 "Accelerator" investor, Martin Hartono," [Electrek posted on X](https://x.com/ElectrekCo/status/2123440464078504385) on Thursday. "It comes a week after Aptera reported $9.2 million in cash against the roughly $25 million it says it needs for series production."

## The first car

According to [Electrek's report](https://electrek.co/2026/11/19/aptera-sev-first-solar-ev-customer-delivery-investor/), the car is finished in "Noir", a matte black exterior, and was built in Carlsbad, California, for Accelerator #1. Accelerators are investors in Aptera's community funding programme, where delivery priority goes to whoever invests the most. Hartono is chief executive of GDP Venture, the Jakarta-based venture arm of Indonesia's Djarum Group.

Aptera did not say which country the car is going to, what Hartono paid, or when it will be handed over, Electrek says. [Teslarati described it](https://x.com/Teslarati/status/2123457406441013457) as a first customer delivery; Aptera's own [announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/19/3406502/0/en/aptera-completes-its-first-solar-electric-vehicle-for-customer-delivery.html) calls it its first production solar electric vehicle completed for customer delivery.

## The money question

Electrek says Aptera ended September with $9.2 million in cash, $4.1 million of which is refundable reservation fees, and lost $14 million in the quarter. Its filing says substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern "has not been alleviated". On 10 November it priced a $6 million registered direct offering at $1.75 a share.

Seven weeks ago, Aptera cut its estimated cost to start production to about $25 million and said the first 40 production vehicles would start building by the end of 2026, with customer deliveries in early 2027 "subject to financing", according to Electrek.

## The solar claim

In August, TÜV Rheinland measured 4.23 to 4.75 kWh a day going into the battery from the car's integrated panels in Carlsbad, which Electrek says works out to roughly 40 miles of daily driving. That relies on Aptera's 100 Wh per mile efficiency target, which the release itself says is not yet confirmed for production vehicles.

## Our read

Analysis: one car for the biggest investor is a milestone, not a production line. The test is the first 40 cars and whether they reach ordinary reservation holders, and that depends on raising most of the $25 million first.
