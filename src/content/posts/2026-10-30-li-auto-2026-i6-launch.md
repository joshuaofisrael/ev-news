Li Auto has launched the 2026 version of its i6 electric SUV at a retail price of 249,800 yuan, the same as the 2025 model, and it now uses the company's own driver-assistance chip and battery. The Chinese carmaker announced the launch in a [release](https://ir.lixiang.com/news-releases/news-release-details/li-auto-inc-launches-2026-li-i6-pioneering-five-seat-suv/) on 28 October. [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/28/li-auto-launches-2026-i6/) reports that buyers who order on or before 30 November get introductory benefits that bring the price to 239,800 yuan, and that the five-seat SUV's CLTC range rises to as much as 780 km.

CnEVPost converts the retail price to about $37,190. Prices and offers apply in China.

## Chip and sensors

The biggest change is the driver-assistance computer. According to CnEVPost, the 2026 i6 comes standard with Li Auto's in-house Mach M100 chip, which replaces the Nvidia Thor-U used in the 2025 car. It says each M100 delivers 1,280 TOPS of computing power, against 700 TOPS for the Thor-U. TOPS, or trillions of operations per second, is the industry's usual headline measure for these chips, although it does not on its own tell a driver how well a system works.

Buyers can choose a package with two M100 chips, for 2,560 TOPS. CnEVPost says that option adds two side-facing and one rear-facing solid-state lidar sensors to the standard forward-facing lidar, plus sensor-activated power front doors.

## Battery and range

The i6 now uses Li Auto's Lianshan 5C lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery, CnEVPost reports, with capacity rising to 96 kWh from 87.3 kWh. CLTC range is 780 km for the rear-wheel-drive version and 710 km for the all-wheel-drive version, increases of 60 km and 50 km. CLTC is China's official test cycle and gives higher figures than the EPA or WLTP tests used in the United States and Europe.

Li Auto says the car can add about 500 km of range in 10 minutes of charging, according to CnEVPost. That is the company's figure, and real charging speed depends on the charger and conditions.

This desk reported on Li Auto's [811 km urban range test of the 2026 i6](/articles/2026-10-26-li-auto-i6-range-test/) before the launch.

## Cabin

CnEVPost says the cabin chip moves from Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8295P to the Snapdragon 8797, and that the two 15.7-inch front displays are replaced by a single 29-inch 6K panoramic screen. Both front seats now come with 16-way power adjustment and massage as standard. Other changes listed include matrix headlights, a powered front trunk, and new exterior door handles. Li Auto also plans to offer a paid powered-frunk upgrade for the 2025 i6 in December, CnEVPost says.

## Deliveries and production

The launch follows a sharp drop in i6 deliveries. CnEVPost, using its own compiled data, says the i6 delivered 7,379 vehicles in September, down 56.54 percent from August, and 160,221 in the first nine months of 2026.

In a [follow-up report](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/30/li-auto-i6-production-200000/) on 30 October, CnEVPost says Li Xinyang, the head of Li Auto's product line, announced on Weibo that the i6 has reached 200,000 units of cumulative production, about 13 months after it first went on sale. He said deliveries of the 2026 model began across China on 29 October, that the current wait for new orders is seven to eight weeks, and that i6 capacity will reach 20,000 units in November. CnEVPost notes that the 200,000 figure counts vehicles built, not delivered.

The two reports describe waits differently. At launch, CnEVPost said Li Auto had opened orders for cars with short lead times, with delivery possible in one to two weeks. Two days later, Li's Weibo post gave seven to eight weeks for orders placed now. The follow-up report says Li Auto prepared vehicles for near-term delivery in advance, which may account for the shorter early waits. Li Auto has not addressed the difference in the sources cited here.

## Europe

The i6 is also part of Li Auto's push into Europe. CnEVPost says it will be sold there as the Li 6, with launch and deliveries in Norway and the Netherlands planned for the first quarter of 2027.
