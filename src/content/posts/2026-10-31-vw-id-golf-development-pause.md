Volkswagen has paused preliminary development work on the electric ID. Golf, the battery-powered successor to its best-known car, because its Wolfsburg headquarters plant is missing a cost target. "We simply can't afford this work right now," VW brand chief Thomas Schäfer said in an internal interview, according to [heise autos](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Volkswagen-pauses-development-work-on-the-ID-Golf-11506575.html). He stressed that the project is not dead: "We're pausing the work, not canceling it."

## Why VW hit pause

The reports tie the decision to money at Wolfsburg. According to heise and [Reuters](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-10-30/vw-suspends-work-on-electric-golf-as-retrenchment-hits-hq), the plant is about €300 million short of its cost target this year. That figure is the plant's shortfall against its cost target, not the price of the ID. Golf project. Other German plants, including Emden and Zwickau, are described as being on track.

[Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/30/volkswagen-pauses-golf-ev-faces-urgent-need-for-action/), citing an internal memo reviewed by Reuters, reports that management sees an urgent need for action on costs at the headquarters site.

The financial backdrop is tight. Heise reports that operating earnings for VW's Core brand group fell to €4.33 billion in the first nine months of 2026, down from €4.72 billion a year earlier. Heise also reports that the VW passenger car brand earned €813 million, and, citing German broadcaster NDR, that it posted a loss of €182 million in the third quarter.

## What it means for the timeline

The electric Golf has already slipped. Heise reports that in the spring the internal target was a launch around 2030, and that VW now says the car is expected in the "early 2030s". VW's works council, which represents employees, is confident, according to an NDR report cited by heise, that the pause does not mean a delay. VW has not given a firmer date.

The car is set to use VW's Scalable Systems Platform, known as SSP, the group's planned next-generation base for electric models. Heise notes that if work on SSP stalls further, other group models planned for the platform could be affected too.

## Smaller EVs keep moving

The pause does not mean VW is stepping back from small electric cars. Heise reports that VW says it has about 150,000 orders for the ID. Polo and related models, the cheaper electric cars below the Golf in size.

*Analysis:* The order book suggests VW's near-term electric push in Europe rests on those smaller, cheaper models, while the Golf-sized EV waits for the new platform and a cheaper cost base at Wolfsburg. That reading is this desk's interpretation of the reported figures, not a VW statement.

## What we still do not know

VW has not said how long the pause will last, what has to happen for work to restart, or whether the decision changes jobs or investment at Wolfsburg. It has also not published a firm launch date for the ID. Golf.
