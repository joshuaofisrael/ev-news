Ford has started making lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery cells at its BlueOval Battery Park in Marshall, Michigan, and the first cells are on their way to its Louisville Assembly Plant. "Ford is officially in the battery business," chief executive Jim Farley [wrote on X](https://x.com/jimfarley98/status/2123114023810166928) on Wednesday.

## What Farley said

Farley wrote that Ford's team has "started battery cell production at BlueOval Battery Park Michigan" and is "shipping our first LFP prismatic cells to Louisville Assembly Plant where they'll power #FordFathom prototypes". He also claimed Ford will be "the first to deliver LFP batteries in the U.S. for mainstream consumer automotive use". That is Ford's own claim, and the post itself does not say how it is measured.

Farley added that Ford is using the same LFP technology and manufacturing know-how for a new energy storage business, Ford Energy.

## The plant and the numbers

According to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/ford-us-battery-cells-catl-tech.html), cell production at the roughly $3 billion plant started last month. The site is expected to be able to make 40 cells a minute, which CNBC says equals 20 gigawatt-hours a year for EVs and energy storage, down from earlier plans of up to 40 gigawatt-hours. Ford told CNBC it is still early in certifying its processes and plans to ramp up cell output next year.

The cells use technology licensed from CATL. Alan Clarke, Ford's vice president of advanced development projects, told CNBC that licensing from "the biggest in the world" was the fastest route to making low-cost LFP cells in the US. The arrangement has drawn criticism from some lawmakers and from US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy over Ford's ties to CATL, CNBC reports.

## What comes next

[electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/11/19/ford-delivers-first-lfp-batteries-for-test-fitting-in-fathom-prototypes/) reports that Ford plans to add battery pack assembly in Marshall before the Fathom goes on sale in 2027, and that the pack will be built into the vehicle structure. Ford has announced a base price of $28,350 for the Standard-Range Fathom, or $29,945 with the destination charge, electrive says. CNBC puts the Fathom's launch in the fourth quarter of 2027.

We have followed the Fathom from its [first test vehicles in Louisville](/articles/2026-11-10-ford-fathom-louisville-test-vehicles/) to its [home power feature](/articles/2026-10-28-ford-fathom-home-power/). Cells from Ford's own plant are the piece that has to arrive for the sub-$30,000 price to hold.
